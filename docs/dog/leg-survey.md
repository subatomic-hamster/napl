# Dog leg survey and the bio-inspired "-al" leg (LEGSURVEY, 2026-10-19)

User request: the legs are very thin and would not take a directional load; look at real robot dogs and animals and give ours a thicker thigh and a light distal
limb. This document: the survey (every number cited, estimates marked), the structural check of the current printed leg (formulas in `tools/dog_leg_struct.py`),
the proposal, and how to load it. Locomotion Monte Carlo was not run (the LEGS worker on p12-legs evaluates the variant in sim after the merge).

Animals ([Hudson et al. 2011, cheetah and greyhound hindlimb, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3077520)):

- Segment lengths: greyhound femur 228 to 236 mm, tibia 242 to 250 mm (ratio about 0.94); cheetah femur 262 to 294 mm, tibia 262 to 298 mm (about 1.0). Thigh : shank length is
  about 1 : 1, the robots above are 1.0 (Go1/Go2/B2), 1.07 (Mini Cheetah), 0.84 (ANYmal). Our 0.22 / 0.22 is inside that range: nothing in the survey argues for
  changing it, so no length variant was made.
- Bone: femur mid-shaft 16.2 to 16.6 mm (greyhound, 25 to 29 kg) and 21.6 to 23.0 mm (cheetah, 27 to 32 kg), femur mass 146 to 153 g against tibia 110 to 118 g (greyhound).
- Muscle: hindlimb muscle 18.8 % (greyhound) and 19.8 % (cheetah) of body mass, "a classical proximal-to-distal reduction in muscle mass ... most hindlimb muscle close
  to the hip", which "reduces swing inertia"; distal tendons long and light.
- Knee direction [est, general anatomy, no source fetched]: hind-limb knee (stifle) forward, forelimb elbow backward. Our dog has front knees back and rear knees forward: the same.

### Design patterns taken from the survey

1. **Heavy proximal, light distal.** Proximal : distal link mass is 6.8 to 11 for Go1/Go2/B2/Mini Cheetah and the animals' muscle mass follows the same gradient; ours is inverted (0.76).
2. **Motors clustered at the hip, knee driven remotely** (Mini Cheetah, Go1 ratio 1.5, Doggo belt, Spot push rod): ours already has this; keep it.
3. **Thick thigh = the structural and transmission housing**: thigh sections of 24 to 40 mm on the loaded axis in the Unitree URDFs against 16 to 24 mm calves; the thigh is a box, the calf a thin tube.
4. **Thin, stiff, light shank**: 16 mm class calves (Go1 16 x 16, Go2 r 12), carbon-fibre segments on ANYmal. Fits a carbon tube.
5. **Small, compliant, light foot**: sphere r 22 mm / 0.04 kg (Go2) against ours 17.5 mm ball + 22 g core.
6. **Closed (box or tube) sections for torsion**: our open plate + rib has a torsion constant of 1.5 cm4 against 5.2 cm4 for the closed 36 x 60 mm plate shell of the "-al" leg (3.5 x, our calculation below; the bands close the cell).

## 2. Structural check of the current printed leg

Method (`tools/dog_leg_struct.py`, stdlib, `python3 tools/dog_leg_struct.py`): planar stance, thigh and shank 35 deg off vertical, knee angle -70 deg (inside the linkage
range), foot under the hip. W = 11.653 kg x 9.81 = 114.3 N (the "-al" leg is loaded with its own dog's 12.46 kg: all loads x 1.069). The push rod's force P balances the foot force's moment about the knee axis using the real four-bar (`cad/dog/params.py`);
the lower leg's force and moment are then taken about the section (thigh root at 30 mm, shank at the slot top, 30 mm), giving axial N, shear, two bending moments and torsion T.
Stress sigma = N/A + M_sag/Z_sag + M_lat/Z_lat, tau = T/W_t + 1.5 V/A, von Mises sqrt(sigma^2 + 3 tau^2). Deflection: virtual work with a unit foot load (bending of both links

- thigh torsion). Not modelled: stress concentrations, fatigue, joint slack, the actuator output bearing's tilting-moment rating (not published), the abduction angle (taken 0).

Load cases (the user's "directional" case is b): a) 343 N vertical (3 x bw, impact), b) 114 N lateral, c) 114 N fore-aft. The knee-rod force P and the hip moment of the
vertical case exceed what the actuators can give (hip 22 N m, knee 33 N m); the structure is judged at the specified loads anyway, and the safety factor at the actuator limit is roughly 2x
higher for a sagittal-dominated case (scaling of the same calculation, [est]).

State plainly: **the current leg does not survive any of the three load cases**; the thigh plate is the weak link (a 5 mm plate cannot take torque about its own axis or a side load), then
the knee pin bearing and the PLA shank. Moreover the sagittal case at the hip motor's own 22 N m already gives SF about 0.4 on the thigh. The user's concern is correct.
Context that stays open for both legs: the hip housing / abad cup are PLA too and carry the same lateral moment (29 N m) after the thigh; the GIM8108-8 output bearing is rated
225 N axial and 900 N radial only ([datasheet quote in the part file](../../parts/actuators/steadywin-gim8108-8.toml)): the 114 N lateral case is inside the axial rating, but a tilting-moment rating
is not published, so the 29 N m moment on the output bearing is **unverified**.

## 3. Proposal: the "-al" leg (revision 2, Go2-style hollow thigh)

Revision 1 of this variant was a 25.4 x 12.7 mm box tube with the knee push rod hanging outside it and an orange 20 mm tube: strong enough on paper but it neither looked nor worked like the
real animals' and Go2 / Mini Cheetah legs (survey pattern 3: a deep thigh that houses the transmission). Revision 2 replaces it; the part ids and file names are unchanged, so the loader and the
assemblies are the same.

The thigh is now 5.7x heavier and the shank 1.5x heavier, so the mass is proximal-heavy in direction (2.84 against 0.76) but still short of the real robots' 6.8 to 11, and the hip-axis inertia rises by 39 % because
the 217 g thigh has its centre 120 mm below the hip. All BOM prices of the new parts are estimates (no SendCutSend instant quote or CNC quote; the CF tube is the only fetched price: 31.70 EUR/m ex VAT; benchmarks in the part files);
supplier path: plates from SendCutSend (0.063 in and 0.187 in 6061 listed) or similar, heads from JLCCNC / PCBWay / Xometry, CF tube from Easy Composites, PA12-CF print from JLC3DP / Xometry.

Expected effects on the LEGS worker's sim runs [est]: the simulation's links are rigid, so **no speed gain is expected from stiffness**; the leg changes are +0.81 kg total mass (about +6.9 % on stance torque and the pack
current, which already caps the dog at 2.0 to 2.25 m/s with the pack at 114 to 142 A against 120 A) and +39 % swing inertia about the hip (still small against the 22 N m hip: 22 / 0.0139 = about 1,600 rad/s2 at the limit).
The benefit is on hardware: the leg survives side loads, stumbles and landings that the printed leg does not, and tolerates the knee pin lateral couple. If the mass or inertia matters, the first lever is windowing the plates between
s = 60 and 180 mm (SF 10 to 28 there; about -45 g per leg [est] and a visible loss of the closed look), the second is a thinner 1.0 mm plate with a thicker doubler.

Not done / open: the plate windows above; foot compliance (the solid ball is kept); hip housing and abad cup strength (PLA, they carry the same 33 N m lateral moment after the thigh); the output-bearing tilting-moment rating
(not published); the pad and doubler bonds (hand-calc assumes laminates); a quote for the machined parts; the carbon tube 25 x 23 stock (priced from the Easy Composites page, strength row from the 16 mm page).

Deviations from the revision-2 brief: the shell is 36 mm wide, not 40 to 50 (the hip output face to knee motor face is 37 mm and the rod, crank and housing y-stack is unchanged; the depth, 60 to 78 mm, carries the visual and structural
thickness); the shank head is 6061, not printed (lateral moment and pin bearing; the printed parts are the bands and the foot cap); the rear of the shell cannot taper toward the knee (the flexed rod end reaches x -37.5).

## 4. Load the variant

```
design.build("assemblies/dog/dog-al.toml", "parts", out_dir)    # whole dog, four '-al' legs (default dog.toml untouched)
design.build("assemblies/dog/leg-al.toml", "parts", out_dir)    # one leg on the bench
```

`dog-al.toml` is `dog.toml` with the four sub-assemblies pointing at `leg-al.toml`; `leg-al.toml` is `leg.toml` with `dog-thigh-al-l`, `dog-shank-cf-l`, `dog-foot-core-cf`
(the right-hand twins are picked by the sub-assembly mirror). Pinned by `tests/python/test_dog_leg_survey.py` (variant differs only there, kinematics identical, DRC clean,
build mass in 10 to 13 kg, hand-calc SF >= 2 on every case for `-al` and < 1 on the thigh of the printed leg). Regenerate the numbers with `python3 tools/dog_leg_struct.py`; re-run the interference check with `cd src && ../cad-venv/bin/python tools/dog_leg_clearance.py` (exit 0 = no overlap).
Renders compared against the default dog (orbit camera, `studio,edges` style) are kept under `remote-out/` (gitignored, never committed).
