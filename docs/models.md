# Device models (milestone N2)

What `src/mujoco/devices/` simulates, as implemented: the equations, the state, the simplifications, and the tests that
validate each. SI units throughout (temperatures degC, motor `kv` in rpm/V). Source of truth is the code
(`devices.cpp`, `electrical.cpp`, `aero.cpp`, `servo.cpp`, `sensors.cpp`); a mismatch is a bug in this page. Tests are
`devices.<name>` in `tests/cpp/test_devices.cpp` unless said otherwise.

Notation: dt = model timestep; `Kt = Ke = 60 / (2 pi kv)`; `R(T) = r20 (1 + alpha (T - 20))`; `g ~ N(0,1)` from the
device's own RNG; `interp` = piecewise linear, clamped at both ends.

| Solid rocket motor | `rocket.cpp`, between the electrical solve and the aero phase. ## Terrain and wind field

Real terrain for landing scenes: seeded heightfields (`python/napl/terrain.py`), MuJoCo `hfield` contact and rays, a renderer that draws them, and the `wind_field` device (`windfield.cpp`) that makes the wind follow the ground. Tests: `tests/cpp/test_terrain.cpp`, `tests/python/test_terrain.py`.

**Generator.** `generate(kind, size, n, relief, roughness, hurst, seed, pads, rocks)`, kinds `ridge` (Gaussian ridge with a summit; anchor `summit`, `windward`), `mesa` (plateau with a tanh cliff of width `cliff_width`, wobbling edge; anchors `plateau`, `brink`, `lowland`), `ledge` (an asymmetric ridge with a steep lee face; anchor `ledge` 30 % of the relief below the summit), `moorland` (rolling swells + a hill; anchor `hilltop`). Large-scale shape + spectral fBm detail (power spectrum `k^-(2H+2)`, random phases, Voss / Peitgen-Saupe) of RMS `roughness` m. Pads (`PadSpec(name, at, radius, blend, z)`) are flattened exactly: the surface is set to the pad height out to `radius + 1.5` grid cells (so every triangle touching the marked disc is flat, `Pad.flat_radius`) and blended back over `blend` m (smoothstep, cut and fill: a pad on a steep face is a carved ledge). The first pad (or `zero_at`) is the datum, z = 0. Boulders are static ellipsoid geoms off the pads. All synthetic: the shapes are plausible, not surveyed terrain. Resolution: choose `n` so a cell is at most ~1/3 of the smallest pad (257 samples over 1.6 km = 6 m cells; the Skydio pad of 3 m radius really is a flat disc of ~12 m radius at that spacing; 513 samples halve it at 4x the triangles).

**Physics.** `add_terrain(root, terrain, wind=..., origin_alt=...)` (or `compose(..., terrain=, wind_field=)`) writes `<hfield>` + one static `hfield` geom `terrain` (contype / conaffinity 1, group 0): bodies with contype 1 rest on it; `mj_ray` and the rangefinders / ground-effect rays see it (vehicle geoms are group 4, outside the default ray mask); pad markers are group 4 (visible, no contact, invisible to rays). MuJoCo's hfield data row 0 is the +y edge; the generator and the kernel account for it. MuJoCo's contact surface is the triangulated cell (diagonal fixed by MuJoCo), not the bilinear patch: the two differ inside a cell by the local curvature (zero on pads). The ISA density follows `origin_alt` (m MSL of world z = 0, the environment device, valid -1000..10000) plus the local z, so a scene at 3200 m has 0.91 kg/m^3 at the pad and thinner air on the summit.

**Renderer.** `Solver::visuals()` triangulates an hfield geom into a `GeomType::hfield` mesh (smooth normals); the renderer shades it by slope and height (meadow, tundra, rock on steep faces, snow on the high gentle ground; stylised, not a land-cover model), with sun shadows, haze (fades farther than 100 m, light enough to read ridges at 1 km) and no crease edges. The shadow map is fitted to the other geoms (vehicles, pads, boulders), not the terrain: a ridge casts shadows near the vehicle but not across the valley. The far plane is 5000 m when the scene has a heightfield (1000 m otherwise: older renders are bit for bit unchanged); the heightfield ends at its edge (sky beyond), so keep cameras inside the map. The mesh is rebuilt and hashed per render (as every mesh): 257 x 193 samples cost a few ms, 513 x 385 tens of ms.

**Wind field** (`wind_fields: [{name, hfield_geom, ground_z, z0, z_ref, h_max, ridge_gain, ridge_len, lee_turb, lee_down}]`, one, needs the environment device; the JSON defaults are `z0 = 0` (no profile), `ridge_gain = 0`, `ridge_len = 80`, `lee_turb = 2`, `lee_down = 0.2`; the Python `WindField` dataclass defaults to `z0 = 0.03`, `ridge_gain = 1`). `Environment.wind` becomes the steady wind at `z_ref` (10 m) above the ground; the wind used by each rotor (hub) and drag body (CoM) is
`W_h + ridge + gust + g_lee turbulence`:
* Boundary layer: `W_h = W ln(h / z0) / ln(z_ref / z0)` on x, y, `h` = height above the hfield surface under the point (bilinear), clamped to `[2 z0, h_max]` (neutral log law; Wieringa 1992 roughness lengths: short grass 0.03 m, rock / snow 0.001-0.01 m); `z0 = 0` = uniform in height.
* Ridge flow (ESTIMATE, linear potential flow over low hills, Jackson & Hunt 1975 / Hunt, Leibovich & Richards 1988): for terrain `h = a cos(kx)` in a wind `U` along x, `u' = U k a cos(kx) e^(-kz)`, `w' = U (dh/dx) e^(-kz)`. A general terrain uses `h_L`, `h_B` (the surface Gaussian-smoothed over `ridge_len` L and 4 L), `u = wind_xy / abs(wind_xy)`, `d = exp(-h / L)`: speed-up `dU = gain U d clamp((h_L - h_B) / L, -0.5, 1)` along `u`, vertical wind `w = gain U d clamp(u . grad h_L, -1, 1)`. So: updraft on windward slopes, downdraft on lee slopes, speed-up on crests, decaying over a height L. One decay height for every scale; nonlinear effects (flow separation, stable-layer lee waves) are not modelled except for the lee term below.
* Lee separation (ESTIMATE): the factor `f = max_k ramp((-u . grad h_L at p - k L u) from 0.15 to 0.4) (1 - k/4) e^(-h / 2L)`, k = 0..3, adds a downdraft `-lee_down U f` (0.2) and multiplies the Dryden turbulence by `1 + (lee_turb - 1) f` (2). The 0.15-0.4 slope ramp brackets Hunt's separation onset near slope 0.3; the gains are not fitted to measurements.
* Dryden turbulence (MIL-F-8785C, as above) now uses the height above the LOCAL ground under `ref_body`.
* Discrete gusts: input `gust` = `[amplitude m/s (signed), H m, dx, dy, dz, delay s, speed m/s]` (7 values, consumed by the next tick; direction need not be unit; up to 8 gusts, oldest overwritten), `gust_clear = 1` drops all. The shape is the FAR 25.341(a) / MIL-F-8785C "1 - cosine": `g(s) = (A / 2)(1 - cos(pi s / H))` for `0 <= s <= 2H` (peak A at `s = H`, total length 2H, integral `A H`), along the unit direction. `s = c (t - t0) - n . (x, y)` for a front travelling at `c = speed > 0` m/s along `n` (the horizontal part of the direction, else of the wind, else +x; it reaches the world origin at `t0`, a point `x` downstream `x / c` later); `speed = 0`: uniform in space with `s = max(abs(wind_xy), 2 m/s) (t - t0)` (Taylor's frozen field, the 2 m/s floor as the Dryden filter's). Outputs: `wind_ref` (3, the full wind at `ref_body`), `height_agl`, `ground`, `lee`, `time`, `n_gusts`.
* Flat terrain (or no hfield), `z0 = 0`, `ridge_gain = 0`, no gust: the field is exactly `wind + turbulence` of the environment device (test `terrain.flat_field_equals_uniform_wind`, bitwise on the drag force). Without a `wind_field` device the kernel is unchanged and every older replay hash holds. The saved state gains one group (`WindState`, last), empty without the device.

Limits: the airframe's thrust tables start at `J = 0` (no negative advance ratio), so a vertical updraft is felt through body drag and the rotor H-force but not as a thrust change, while a downdraft (positive inflow along the thrust axis) costs thrust as it does in reality; the wind acts on rotors and body drag only (not on lift surfaces, pellets and rocket airframes keep the uniform `wind_at`); no thermal convection, no stratified (stable) lee waves, no time evolution of the mean wind; the log law needs neutral stability and `h_max` caps it at 300 m.

## Rain

A `rain` device (schema `Rain`, `src/mujoco/devices/rain.cpp`; effects in `aero.cpp`, `rangefinder.cpp`; look in `src/render`). One per model, **opt-in**: a model without it has the old state layout and replay hashes (the group is appended last in the saved state); with it, `rate = 0` (the default) changes nothing bitwise. `rate` (mm/h, 0..500) is the only input and is settable at run time (`sim.set("/devices/rain", "rate", R)`, logged and replayed like any command; a time schedule is the harness' loop, there is no kernel-side table). Python: `Environment(rain=True, rain_rate=..., rain_vehicle=..., rain_catch_area=..., rain_wet_area=...)` in `cuas.scene.compose`, or a `type = "rain"` binding in a design (`FIELD_UNITS` has the fields).

**Rain field.** Marshall-Palmer drops, `N(D) = 8000 exp(-4.1 R^-0.21 D)` m^-3 mm^-1 (Marshall and Palmer 1948, as quoted in Rasshofer, Spies and Spies 2011, eq. 21). Liquid water content `LWC = 0.072 R^0.88` g/m^3 (the power-law fit of the project brief; the Marshall-Palmer integral is `0.089 R^0.84` (recalled, not re-fetched), within 25 % at 1 mm/h and 3 % at 100). Fall speed `v = R / LWC` with `R` converted to a mass flux (1 mm/h = 1 kg m^-2 h^-1), so the water flux through a horizontal plane is exactly `R`: 3.9 m/s at 1 mm/h, 5.7 at 25, 6.7 at 100. Cross-check, Atlas-Ulbrich `v = 3.78 D^0.67` at the mass-weighted `D_m = 0.976 R^0.21` mm: 3.7 / 5.9 / 7.1 m/s. Drops fall at `(wind_x, wind_y, wind_z - v)`: **they drift with the steady wind + turbulence of the environment device** (no inertia lag: a 1-2 mm drop relaxes to the wind in ~0.3 s). Air density is not changed by rain (the water is < 0.5 % of the air mass at 100 mm/h). Outputs `lwc` (g/m^3), `v_fall` (m/s), `film_mass` (kg), `force` (N), `thrust_loss` (mean fractional thrust loss of the air rotors, last tick).

**Rotors and ducted fans** (all non-`water` rotors; the EDF of the hopper is a rotor, so it gets the same model: *flagged approximation*, the duct protects the blades from the side but the fan sees the whole inflow). With `x = LWC / 23 g/m^3`:
`T *= max(0.5, 1 - rain_kt x)`, `Q *= 1 + rain_kq x`, defaults `rain_kt = 0.10`, `rain_kq = 0.15` per rotor (schema `Rotor`). Source of the form: wetted/rain-laden airfoils lose lift and gain drag (Bezos et al., NASA TP-3184, 1992: NACA 64-210 at LWC 16-46 g/m^3, losses in maximum lift and drag rise for a given lift, the largest observed in the NASA rain studies 27 % lift loss and 39 % drag rise on a high-lift configuration near maximum lift; the mechanism is roughened wetted surface, droplet momentum loss and earlier separation). 23 g/m^3 is the bottom of that tunnel range (~1000 mm/h equivalent); the coefficients scale the tunnel's order of magnitude (about 10 % lift, 15 % drag/torque at 23 g/m^3) down linearly to flight LWC. **Estimate**: published propeller-in-rain data is thin (the Iowa State rain/icing tunnel work and ICAS 2020-0482 report that heavy rain is less severe for a quadcopter than gusts or downdraft, without a usable coefficient), so `rain_kt`, `rain_kq` are the knobs to tune against a thrust stand; the Skydio test prints the consequence (below). Drop momentum on the disk: `F = 0.5 LWC A_disk |u_ax| u_ax` along the axis, `u_ax` = (drop velocity - hub velocity) . axis (the 0.5 is the intercepted fraction of the swept disk, an estimate); about 5 mN per rotor at 100 mm/h (negligible next to the 15 N hover thrust; the blade-penalty term above dominates).

**Film body** (`body`, `catch_area`, `wet_area`; typically the vehicle root). `u = drop velocity - CoM velocity`. Catch force `F = LWC A_c |u| u` at the CoM (drops lose all relative momentum: inelastic; splash and rebound, up to 2x, ignored). Water film mass `dm/dt = collect_eff LWC A_c |u| - m / film_tau`, `m <= 1000 film_depth wet_area`; defaults `collect_eff = 0.3`, `film_tau = 20 s` (blow-off in the downwash plus dripping), `film_depth = 0.3 mm` (all three **estimates**), `catch_area` / `wet_area` are the caller's (X500: 0.09 / 0.25 m^2). The film's weight `m g` acts at the CoM; it adds no inertia and does not shift the CoM (fine for the few grams to 90 g of a 1.7 kg drone). Body drag in rain: the catch force; the existing `body_drag` device (air drag) is unchanged.

**Range sensors** (`rain_*` on a Rangefinder; kernel defaults = a 905 nm lidar / ToF; the design builder gives pinhole depth cameras `rain_margin_db = 4`, `rain_det = 0.02`, **estimates**: active-stereo matching degrades before a ToF pulse does). Extinction of Marshall-Palmer rain with `Q_ext = 2` (large drops, geometric optics; Rasshofer et al. eq. 19, `alpha = pi/8 integral D^2 Q_ext N dD`): `alpha = pi N0 / (2 L^3) = 1.82e-4 R^0.63` 1/m (`rain_a`, `rain_b`) = 0.18 R^0.63 /km (6 dB/km at 25 mm/h; derived here from the integral, not fitted to a measurement; Rasshofer et al. report the maximum detection range of 905 nm automotive sensors falling with rain rate up to 18 mm/h in an indoor rain simulator, without a figure I could extract). Per true return at range `r`: two-way loss `L = 8.686 alpha r + rain_window_db sqrt(R / 25)` dB (the second term is the water film on the sensor window and wet targets: **estimate**, 1.5 dB at 25 mm/h); detection probability `p(M) = 1 / (1 + exp(-M / 1.5 dB))` with link margin `M = rain_margin_db + 20 log10(max_range / r)`; the return is dropped with probability `1 - p(M - L) / p(M)` (0 at `R = 0`), and the range noise `sigma` is multiplied by `10^(L/20)` (amplitude SNR). False returns of drops in the beam, per ray per frame: `P = 1 - exp(-n A (rain_zone - min_range) rain_det)`, `n = 1951 R^0.21` drops/m^3 (`N0 / Lambda`), `A = pi/4 rain_beam^2`, `rain_det` = fraction of drops that give a detectable echo (**estimate**; 0 turns them off); a false return is uniform in `[min_range, rain_zone]` and replaces the true one when nearer. No RNG is drawn at `R = 0`. Result for the 160x90 D435i-class camera at a wall 8 m away (`tests/python/test_rain.py`): valid pixels 1.00 / 0.87 / 0.64 and false near returns 0 / 2.9 % / 3.9 % at 0 / 25 / 100 mm/h. **Camera images**: the kernel has no camera image model (the `Camera` device is a pose; images come from the renderer, which now draws the rain), so detectors that work on rendered images see the rain look automatically and nothing else is modelled. **GNSS, IMU, barometer, magnetometer are unaffected** (L-band / inertial; barometer: rain does not change static pressure at this fidelity).

**Renderer** (`RenderStyle::rain`, `overcast`; `Solver::weather()` -> `Weather{rain, wind, time}`; the viewer passes it per frame). Outdoor sky only. Rain 0 and no `rain=` / `overcast=` words = the clear look bit for bit (`render.rain_look_changes_with_the_rate_and_clear_is_unchanged`). Overcast `o = 1 - exp(-R/5)` (0.18 at 1, 0.99 at 25 mm/h) or `overcast=<0..1>`; sky stops, sun (x `1 - 0.85 o`, no glare, no disc), ambient (x `1 - 0.2 o`), ground albedo (x `1 - 0.3 o`, wet) move toward grey; haze density `+ 0.0016 sqrt(R)` per m; clouds become a complete grey deck. Streaks: `14000 (1 - exp(-R/30))` instanced quads, one per drop in a 48 m world-fixed periodic box around the camera moving with `(wind, -v_fall)` (so parallax, wind slant and speed are right), screen-space width 1.5 px, exposure length `0.06 s |v|`, alpha-blended and depth-tested against the scene. **Art-directed, not radiometric**: the sky/ground/haze numbers are chosen to read as rain, not derived from a sky model; real rain scenes also show spray and splash that are not drawn. Style words: `rain=<mm/h>`, `overcast=<0..1>`, `rain=auto`, `overcast=auto`.

Tests: `tests/cpp/test_rain.cpp` (closed forms, thrust-loss monotone and small, dry device bitwise, film cap and drain, lidar dropout / noise / false-return monotone), `tests/python/test_rain.py` (Skydio hover at 0 / 25 / 100 mm/h, depth statistics, schema), `render.rain_look_*`.

## Qdd

A `qdd` device (schema `Qdd`, `src/mujoco/devices/qdd.cpp`): an integrated-driver quasi-direct-drive actuator (BLDC + planetary gear + FOC driver, MIT-mode command protocol over CAN) on the gear **output** hinge. **Opt-in** and appended last in the saved state: models without it keep their layout and hashes. Every physical parameter must come from the part (a missing one fails the load, naming the device); `docs/models.md` is the only place the model constants live, and the part files carry the provenance. Bind with `type = "qdd"` (`instance`, power `port`, `joint`, `map`/`set`); `auto_devices` maps the params of `actuator.bldc.qdd` parts (`python/napl/design/auto_devices.py`: `QDD_MOTOR`, `QDD`).

**Command path.** Settable attrs `p_des` (rad), `v_des` (rad/s), `kp` (N m/rad), `kd` (N m s/rad), `tau_ff` (N m), `enable`. They are sampled every `1/cmd_hz` (tick % period == 0) and become effective `latency_s` later (integer ticks; a ring of 16 samples bounds it to 14 periods). Before the first effective sample the driver is disabled. A `set` of any of them counts as a received CAN frame (`rx_tick`); a host that stops writing for more than `timeout_s` while enabled trips the driver's timeout (the age is measured at the driver: write tick + latency): `timeout_mode 0` damping, `tau = -kd_timeout dq` (the CubeMars / Steadywin-style "exit to damping"; `kd_timeout` is a per-part number), `1` open (zero current, free joint). `kp`, `kd`, `tau_ff` are clamped to `[0, kp_max]`, `[0, kd_max]`, `+-tau_ff_max` (the MIT frame's fixed-point ranges).

**Torque.** `tau_cmd = kp (p_des - q) + kd (v_des - dq) + tau_ff` with `q` the output encoder angle quantised to `encoder_cpr` counts and `dq` the ideal joint velocity (no differentiation noise or filter, no sensing delay beyond the sample-and-hold of the tick). The torque loop is ideal (a current loop much faster than the tick). `i_q = tau_cmd / (Kt N k)`, `Kt = Ke = 60 / (2 pi kv)` (DC-equivalent winding, as the ESC motor), `k = eta` while the motor delivers power and `1` when back-driven, ramped over `tanh(w / w_s)` exactly like `drive_motor` (a modelling regularisation, not a gearbox efficiency curve). `i_q` is then limited, in this order of authority:

- current: `|i_q| <= i_limit x derate(T)`;
- voltage (the averaged FOC torque-speed envelope): `|R(T) i_q + Ke w| <= V_bus`, `w = N dq` (motor shaft), `V_bus` = the node voltage of this tick. This is the linear SVPWM limit: the maximum phase voltage amplitude is `V_bus / sqrt(3)`, i.e. line-to-line peak `V_bus`, which is the convention of the datasheet `Kv`, `Ke` and line-to-line `R` (so the no-load output speed is `V_bus / (Ke N)`: 33.5 rad/s = 320 rpm at 48 V for the AK70-9, the published value). The torque therefore falls linearly from the stall value to zero at the no-load speed. A command above the envelope saturates to it, never exceeds it. No field weakening; no inductance (a real driver's current-loop bandwidth, `L/R` = 0.9 ms for the AK70-9, and the PWM ripple are not modelled);
- beyond the no-load speed the feasible set is empty: `i_q` is the voltage bound, a regenerative brake (the diodes of a real inverter), capped at `i_limit`.

Output torque on the joint: `tau_est = Kt N k i_q` minus the motor loss torque `N loss(w)` (`MotorParams` `i0` model; applied also when the driver is disabled). The reflected rotor inertia `J_rotor N^2` is SET as the joint's `dof_armature` at bind (idempotent), `dof_damping` is 0 (a FOC driver with `i_q = 0` has no back-EMF drag). Not modelled: gearbox backlash and elasticity (put a `backlash_joint`-style hinge in the MJCF if needed), cogging, cable stiffness.

**Thermal.** `C_th dT/dt = i_q^2 R(T) - (T - T_amb) / R_th` (explicit Euler, winding only; iron and friction heat is not counted). `derate = clamp((t_max - T) / (t_max - derate_start), 0, 1)` multiplies the current limit; `fault` latches at `T >= t_max` (torque off) and clears once `enable = 0` and `T < t_max`. With a linear foldback to 0 the winding settles below `t_max` under any constant load; the fault is reached only through a heat step larger than the foldback band (tiny `c_th`, as the test uses) or an ambient above the derate band. The derate thresholds are the part's (`temp_derate_start`, `temp_fault`).

**Bus.** `bus_i = (R i_q + Ke w) i_q / V_bus` (electrical power of the DC-equivalent winding over the node voltage; lossless inverter, no quiescent draw; negative = regeneration). It is injected as a constant sink into the **next** tick's electrical solve (`solve_electrical` runs first because the voltage limit needs this tick's node voltage): a one-tick lag (1 ms), harmless at the supply impedances of a battery (loop gain `r_series P / V^2` << 1). Battery sag under 12 joints is therefore represented.

Read attrs: `q`, `dq`, `tau_est`, `tau_cmd`, `i_q`, `temp`, `fault`, `bus_i`, `voltage`, `derate`, `timeout`.

Tests: `tests/cpp/test_qdd.cpp` (stall = `min(i_limit, V/R) Kt N eta` within 1 %; torque-speed sweep at 24 / 48 V against the closed form to 1e-6 and the published no-load speed within 5 %; kp/kd step; latency exact at two command rates; timeout damping / open and frames keeping it alive; thermal rise and derate; fault latch; limits and encoder grid; save/restore/clone bitwise; missing parameter), `tests/python/test_qdd.py` (API units and read-only, the design builder and unit check).

## FootContact

A `foot_contact` device (schema `FootContact`, `src/mujoco/devices/contact.cpp`), per foot geom. `force` = the sum, over **all** contacts that involve the geom (any partner), of the solved normal contact force (`mj_contactForce`, component 0; zero for a contact without a constraint row), of the tick just stepped (the state `s_k` the tick started from, like every sensor); plus `noise_std` N(0,1) from the device's own RNG (drawn only when `noise_std > 0`), clipped to `[0, f_max]`; held between `rate_hz` samples. `contact` is a comparator with hysteresis (on above `hysteresis_on`, off at or below `hysteresis_off`) and a debounce: a change must persist `debounce_s` (the first differing sample starts the clock, the output follows when the elapsed time reaches `debounce_s`). `mode` (0 force sensor, 1 binary switch) is metadata. Not modelled: sensor bandwidth, creep, temperature drift, shear sensitivity (a strain-gauge foot also reads tangential load), the compliance of the foot pad beyond the MuJoCo contact softness. Tests: `tests/cpp/test_foot_contact.cpp` (rock + hfield seam: sum of two contacts equals the weight and the independent contact sum; saturation; debounce timing both edges; sample-and-hold; seeded noise replay; bad config), `tests/python/test_foot_contact.py`.

## Rosette scan pattern

`projection: "rosette"` on a rangefinder (a Livox Mid-360-like non-repeating scan). Documented approximation: the real pattern is proprietary; what is modelled is the published envelope (360 deg horizontal, -7..52 deg vertical, 200 000 points/s, 0.1 m to 40 m at 10 % reflectivity). Ray `m = frames*rays_per_frame + k` points along the R2 low-discrepancy sequence (plastic-number generalised golden ratio) offset by `pattern_seed`: azimuth uniform on `fov_h`, elevation uniform in angle between `fov_v_min` and `fov_v_max` (so the ring density per solid angle rises towards the top of the span; `examples/dog/autonomy/lidar.py` mirrors the sequence). Spherical frame, +x of the site is the elevation axis; the image is 1 x `rays_per_frame`. `rays_per_frame = 20000` at 10 Hz is the real point rate; fewer is a cheaper approximation. Tests: `tests/cpp/test_rosette.cpp` (rays in the FoV, >= 95 % of 1 x 1 deg bins covered after 1 s without repeating, analytic floor / ceiling ranges, bitwise replay, self-hit exclusion by the group mask).

Cost (hfbox, one 2001 x 2001 hfield, whole step): about 1500-2000 rays/s, independent of `max_range`. Tiled hfield patches are measured in "Ground sim budget" below.

## Small-scale terrain

`napl.terrain.field(kind, size=100, res=0.05, seed)` (`python/napl/terrain_field.py`) makes 100 x 100 m at 5 cm of `rubble` (5-20 cm rock bumps plus boulder boxes), `slopes` (15-30 deg ramps, 10-18 cm steps), `scree` (small stones, low-friction patches) or `ruins` (wall maze with 0.6-1.6 m doors). `add_field(xml, f)` emits one hfield geom per 10 m patch (201 x 201, neighbours share their border row so there are no seams) and yaw-only box geoms. Per-patch `Material` (rock 0.8, gravel 0.55, grass 0.6, wet_rock 0.35, mud 0.25 sliding friction; every value `estimated`, sources in `MATERIALS`) sets `friction` and `solref` with `priority=1`, so the surface friction replaces the foot's instead of the MuJoCo max rule. `solref` 0.02-0.04 s, `solimp` default: the contact time constant of at least 10 timesteps at dt 2 ms, and a 12 kg dummy dog rests within 1 mm and does not tunnel at dt 1 and 2 ms on all four kinds. Ground truth: `f.height(x, y)` is the MuJoCo-triangulated hfield (cell diagonal `DIAG`) max the box tops, equal to a ray cast within 1 mm; `f.material_at(x, y)`. Same seed gives the same `digest()` across processes. Tests: `tests/python/test_terrain_field.py`.

## Tick order

`devs::step` replaces `mj_step` for a model with devices, in this fixed order:

1. zero `dof_damping` of every driven dof (so `qfrc_passive` is computed with `b_old = 0` whatever a restore left in the model);
2. `mj_step1`; zero `qfrc_applied` and `xfrc_applied` (the devices own them);
3. sensors-read (the held samples already sit in the state: nothing consumes them yet) and controllers: servo encoder (every tick) and firmware (on its period);
4. shaft speeds from `qvel` (servo: `N qvel`), electrical solve (QDD bus currents of the previous tick enter as sinks); then `step_qdd` (command intake, FOC limits at this tick's bus voltage, torque, thermal);
5. motors (`drive_motor`: damping split, thermal);
6. aero / forces: environment (turbulence), rotors, body drag; then launchers (spawn on `fire`, RK4 flight, swept hit test, mocap visuals) and camera poses (`step_launchers`);
7. `mj_step2`;
8. post-step hook: sensors due this tick sample (IMU / baro / mag / GNSS, range sensors, foot contacts).

Rates of firmware and sensors must give an integer number of ticks per step (`1 / (rate dt)`), else the load fails.

## Sensor timing

`mj_step2` integrates `qpos` and `qvel`, but position, velocity and acceleration derived data (`site_xpos`, `site_xmat`, `cvel`, and the `qacc` it solved) still describe the state `s_k` the tick started from. Samples are computed in the post-step hook from derived data only, so all of one sample refers to `s_k`. Acceleration needs `mj_rnePostConstraint`, which MuJoCo runs only for models with acceleration sensors (and then before integrating): when it has not run, the IMU runs it itself, with `qvel` temporarily set back to the value saved before `mj_step2`. A sample is visible after tick `k` completes, i.e. one dt after the state it describes (plus the GNSS latency), and held until the next sample tick. The first tick samples (`ticks % period == 0`); before it outputs read 0 (GNSS: its origin).

## Determinism

Everything that changes from tick to tick is in the saved state: `mjSTATE_INTEGRATION` + actuator gain / bias + one `std::vector<State>` per device type (battery, psu, wire, load, esc, rotor, env, drag, servo, imu, baro, mag, gnss, then solid motor, airframe, sea, vessel, rangefinder, in that order (later groups appended last so older hashes are unchanged), plain doubles / uint64 without padding, `memcpy`'d) + the structure id. That covers inputs (throttle, goals, wind, enable), outputs, thermal / RC / filter / integrator / bias states, tick counters, the GNSS ring and every RNG. `save`, `restore`, `clone`, `state_hash` and replay therefore agree bitwise. Not saved because they carry no state across ticks: the device configs (re-parsed from the model), `dof_damping` of driven dofs (zeroed before use) and the solver scratch. RNG: xoshiro256** seeded with splitmix64, one stream per noisy device (`seed` of the device, else `Devices.seed + flat slot index`); `gauss()` is our own Box-Muller (two uniforms per sample, no cached spare) because `std::normal_distribution` is not specified bitwise. Tests: `save_restore_bitwise`, `save_restore_bitwise_aero`, `servo_save_restore_bitwise`, `all_save_restore_clone_bitwise` (mid-run restore off every sample / firmware tick, `all.xml`: every device type in one free-floating quad), `all_bus_log_and_replay`, `all_free_quad_runs_without_divergence`, `model_constants_follow_device_armature_and_are_idempotent`, and the unchanged `test_determinism` / `test_replay`.

Model constants: when an ESC or servo sets `dof_armature`, `bind()` refreshes every qpos0-derived constant (`dof_M0`, `dof_invweight0`, ...) once with `mj_setConst` on a scratch `mjData`: idempotent, safe on a reloaded model and after Spawn / Delete.

## Config transport

Devices are declared in the MJCF as `<custom><text name="napl.devices" data='{...json...}'/></custom>`. The JSON follows `schema/devices.fbs` (kept apart from `napl.fbs`, whose hash is in replay logs) and is parsed with `flatbuffers::Parser` (strict JSON: quoted names, an unknown field is an error) using the embedded binary schema, then copied into plain config structs; any error names the device (`napl.devices: device '<name>': ...`). `mj_saveModel` keeps custom text, so replay headers, `load_mujoco_bytes` and `clone` carry the configuration; every load path ends in the `MujocoSolver` constructor, which parses and binds. Device names become prims `/devices/<name>`; attributes and units are the tables at the end of `devices.cpp` (inputs are settable through `SetAttr`, validated before anything is written).
