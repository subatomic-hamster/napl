# Device models (milestone N2)

What `src/mujoco/devices/` simulates, as implemented: the equations, the state, the simplifications, and the tests that
validate each. SI units throughout (temperatures degC, motor `kv` in rpm/V). Source of truth is the code
(`devices.cpp`, `electrical.cpp`, `aero.cpp`, `servo.cpp`, `sensors.cpp`); a mismatch is a bug in this page. Tests are
`devices.<name>` in `tests/cpp/test_devices.cpp` unless said otherwise.

Notation: dt = model timestep; `Kt = Ke = 60 / (2 pi kv)`; `R(T) = r20 (1 + alpha (T - 20))`; `g ~ N(0,1)` from the
device's own RNG; `interp` = piecewise linear, clamped at both ends.

| Solid rocket motor | `rocket.cpp`, between the electrical solve and the aero phase. ## Rain

A `rain` device (schema `Rain`, `src/mujoco/devices/rain.cpp`; effects in `aero.cpp`, `rangefinder.cpp`; look in `src/render`). One per model, **opt-in**: a model without it has the old state layout and replay hashes (the group is appended last in the saved state); with it, `rate = 0` (the default) changes nothing bitwise. `rate` (mm/h, 0..500) is the only input and is settable at run time (`sim.set("/devices/rain", "rate", R)`, logged and replayed like any command; a time schedule is the harness' loop, there is no kernel-side table). Python: `Environment(rain=True, rain_rate=..., rain_vehicle=..., rain_catch_area=..., rain_wet_area=...)` in `cuas.scene.compose`, or a `type = "rain"` binding in a design (`FIELD_UNITS` has the fields).

**Rain field.** Marshall-Palmer drops, `N(D) = 8000 exp(-4.1 R^-0.21 D)` m^-3 mm^-1 (Marshall and Palmer 1948, as quoted in Rasshofer, Spies and Spies 2011, eq. 21). Liquid water content `LWC = 0.072 R^0.88` g/m^3 (the power-law fit of the project brief; the Marshall-Palmer integral is `0.089 R^0.84` (recalled, not re-fetched), within 25 % at 1 mm/h and 3 % at 100). Fall speed `v = R / LWC` with `R` converted to a mass flux (1 mm/h = 1 kg m^-2 h^-1), so the water flux through a horizontal plane is exactly `R`: 3.9 m/s at 1 mm/h, 5.7 at 25, 6.7 at 100. Cross-check, Atlas-Ulbrich `v = 3.78 D^0.67` at the mass-weighted `D_m = 0.976 R^0.21` mm: 3.7 / 5.9 / 7.1 m/s. Drops fall at `(wind_x, wind_y, wind_z - v)`: **they drift with the steady wind + turbulence of the environment device** (no inertia lag: a 1-2 mm drop relaxes to the wind in ~0.3 s). Air density is not changed by rain (the water is < 0.5 % of the air mass at 100 mm/h). Outputs `lwc` (g/m^3), `v_fall` (m/s), `film_mass` (kg), `force` (N), `thrust_loss` (mean fractional thrust loss of the air rotors, last tick).

**Rotors and ducted fans** (all non-`water` rotors; the EDF of the hopper is a rotor, so it gets the same model: *flagged approximation*, the duct protects the blades from the side but the fan sees the whole inflow). With `x = LWC / 23 g/m^3`:
`T *= max(0.5, 1 - rain_kt x)`, `Q *= 1 + rain_kq x`, defaults `rain_kt = 0.10`, `rain_kq = 0.15` per rotor (schema `Rotor`). Source of the form: wetted/rain-laden airfoils lose lift and gain drag (Bezos et al., NASA TP-3184, 1992: NACA 64-210 at LWC 16-46 g/m^3, losses in maximum lift and drag rise for a given lift, the largest observed in the NASA rain studies 27 % lift loss and 39 % drag rise on a high-lift configuration near maximum lift; the mechanism is roughened wetted surface, droplet momentum loss and earlier separation). 23 g/m^3 is the bottom of that tunnel range (~1000 mm/h equivalent); the coefficients scale the tunnel's order of magnitude (about 10 % lift, 15 % drag/torque at 23 g/m^3) down linearly to flight LWC. **Estimate**: published propeller-in-rain data is thin (the Iowa State rain/icing tunnel work and ICAS 2020-0482 report that heavy rain is less severe for a quadcopter than gusts or downdraft, without a usable coefficient), so `rain_kt`, `rain_kq` are the knobs to tune against a thrust stand; the Skydio test prints the consequence (below). Drop momentum on the disk: `F = 0.5 LWC A_disk |u_ax| u_ax` along the axis, `u_ax` = (drop velocity - hub velocity) . axis (the 0.5 is the intercepted fraction of the swept disk, an estimate); about 5 mN per rotor at 100 mm/h (negligible next to the 15 N hover thrust; the blade-penalty term above dominates).

**Film body** (`body`, `catch_area`, `wet_area`; typically the vehicle root). `u = drop velocity - CoM velocity`. Catch force `F = LWC A_c |u| u` at the CoM (drops lose all relative momentum: inelastic; splash and rebound, up to 2x, ignored). Water film mass `dm/dt = collect_eff LWC A_c |u| - m / film_tau`, `m <= 1000 film_depth wet_area`; defaults `collect_eff = 0.3`, `film_tau = 20 s` (blow-off in the downwash plus dripping), `film_depth = 0.3 mm` (all three **estimates**), `catch_area` / `wet_area` are the caller's (X500: 0.09 / 0.25 m^2). The film's weight `m g` acts at the CoM; it adds no inertia and does not shift the CoM (fine for the few grams to 90 g of a 1.7 kg drone). Body drag in rain: the catch force; the existing `body_drag` device (air drag) is unchanged.

**Range sensors** (`rain_*` on a Rangefinder; kernel defaults = a 905 nm lidar / ToF; the design builder gives pinhole depth cameras `rain_margin_db = 4`, `rain_det = 0.02`, **estimates**: active-stereo matching degrades before a ToF pulse does). Extinction of Marshall-Palmer rain with `Q_ext = 2` (large drops, geometric optics; Rasshofer et al. eq. 19, `alpha = pi/8 integral D^2 Q_ext N dD`): `alpha = pi N0 / (2 L^3) = 1.82e-4 R^0.63` 1/m (`rain_a`, `rain_b`) = 0.18 R^0.63 /km (6 dB/km at 25 mm/h; derived here from the integral, not fitted to a measurement; Rasshofer et al. report the maximum detection range of 905 nm automotive sensors falling with rain rate up to 18 mm/h in an indoor rain simulator, without a figure I could extract). Per true return at range `r`: two-way loss `L = 8.686 alpha r + rain_window_db sqrt(R / 25)` dB (the second term is the water film on the sensor window and wet targets: **estimate**, 1.5 dB at 25 mm/h); detection probability `p(M) = 1 / (1 + exp(-M / 1.5 dB))` with link margin `M = rain_margin_db + 20 log10(max_range / r)`; the return is dropped with probability `1 - p(M - L) / p(M)` (0 at `R = 0`), and the range noise `sigma` is multiplied by `10^(L/20)` (amplitude SNR). False returns of drops in the beam, per ray per frame: `P = 1 - exp(-n A (rain_zone - min_range) rain_det)`, `n = 1951 R^0.21` drops/m^3 (`N0 / Lambda`), `A = pi/4 rain_beam^2`, `rain_det` = fraction of drops that give a detectable echo (**estimate**; 0 turns them off); a false return is uniform in `[min_range, rain_zone]` and replaces the true one when nearer. No RNG is drawn at `R = 0`. Result for the 160x90 D435i-class camera at a wall 8 m away (`tests/python/test_rain.py`): valid pixels 1.00 / 0.87 / 0.64 and false near returns 0 / 2.9 % / 3.9 % at 0 / 25 / 100 mm/h. **Camera images**: the kernel has no camera image model (the `Camera` device is a pose; images come from the renderer, which now draws the rain), so detectors that work on rendered images see the rain look automatically and nothing else is modelled. **GNSS, IMU, barometer, magnetometer are unaffected** (L-band / inertial; barometer: rain does not change static pressure at this fidelity).

**Renderer** (`RenderStyle::rain`, `overcast`; `Solver::weather()` -> `Weather{rain, wind, time}`; the viewer passes it per frame). Outdoor sky only. Rain 0 and no `rain=` / `overcast=` words = the clear look bit for bit (`render.rain_look_changes_with_the_rate_and_clear_is_unchanged`). Overcast `o = 1 - exp(-R/5)` (0.18 at 1, 0.99 at 25 mm/h) or `overcast=<0..1>`; sky stops, sun (x `1 - 0.85 o`, no glare, no disc), ambient (x `1 - 0.2 o`), ground albedo (x `1 - 0.3 o`, wet) move toward grey; haze density `+ 0.0016 sqrt(R)` per m; clouds become a complete grey deck. Streaks: `14000 (1 - exp(-R/30))` instanced quads, one per drop in a 48 m world-fixed periodic box around the camera moving with `(wind, -v_fall)` (so parallax, wind slant and speed are right), screen-space width 1.5 px, exposure length `0.06 s |v|`, alpha-blended and depth-tested against the scene. **Art-directed, not radiometric**: the sky/ground/haze numbers are chosen to read as rain, not derived from a sky model; real rain scenes also show spray and splash that are not drawn. Style words: `rain=<mm/h>`, `overcast=<0..1>`, `rain=auto`, `overcast=auto`.

Tests: `tests/cpp/test_rain.cpp` (closed forms, thrust-loss monotone and small, dry device bitwise, film cap and drain, lidar dropout / noise / false-return monotone), `tests/python/test_rain.py` (Skydio hover at 0 / 25 / 100 mm/h, depth statistics, schema), `render.rain_look_*`.

## Tick order

`devs::step` replaces `mj_step` for a model with devices, in this fixed order:

1. zero `dof_damping` of every driven dof (so `qfrc_passive` is computed with `b_old = 0` whatever a restore left in the model);
2. `mj_step1`; zero `qfrc_applied` and `xfrc_applied` (the devices own them);
3. sensors-read (the held samples already sit in the state: nothing consumes them yet) and controllers: servo encoder (every tick) and firmware (on its period);
4. shaft speeds from `qvel` (servo: `N qvel`), electrical solve;
5. motors (`drive_motor`: damping split, thermal);
6. aero / forces: environment (turbulence), rotors, body drag; then launchers (spawn on `fire`, RK4 flight, swept hit test, mocap visuals) and camera poses (`step_launchers`);
7. `mj_step2`;
8. post-step hook: sensors due this tick sample.

Rates of firmware and sensors must give an integer number of ticks per step (`1 / (rate dt)`), else the load fails.

## Sensor timing

`mj_step2` integrates `qpos` and `qvel`, but position, velocity and acceleration derived data (`site_xpos`, `site_xmat`, `cvel`, and the `qacc` it solved) still describe the state `s_k` the tick started from. Samples are computed in the post-step hook from derived data only, so all of one sample refers to `s_k`. Acceleration needs `mj_rnePostConstraint`, which MuJoCo runs only for models with acceleration sensors (and then before integrating): when it has not run, the IMU runs it itself, with `qvel` temporarily set back to the value saved before `mj_step2`. A sample is visible after tick `k` completes, i.e. one dt after the state it describes (plus the GNSS latency), and held until the next sample tick. The first tick samples (`ticks % period == 0`); before it outputs read 0 (GNSS: its origin).

## Determinism

Everything that changes from tick to tick is in the saved state: `mjSTATE_INTEGRATION` + actuator gain / bias + one `std::vector<State>` per device type (battery, psu, wire, load, esc, rotor, env, drag, servo, imu, baro, mag, gnss, then solid motor, airframe, sea, vessel, rangefinder, in that order (later groups appended last so older hashes are unchanged), plain doubles / uint64 without padding, `memcpy`'d) + the structure id. That covers inputs (throttle, goals, wind, enable), outputs, thermal / RC / filter / integrator / bias states, tick counters, the GNSS ring and every RNG. `save`, `restore`, `clone`, `state_hash` and replay therefore agree bitwise. Not saved because they carry no state across ticks: the device configs (re-parsed from the model), `dof_damping` of driven dofs (zeroed before use) and the solver scratch. RNG: xoshiro256** seeded with splitmix64, one stream per noisy device (`seed` of the device, else `Devices.seed + flat slot index`); `gauss()` is our own Box-Muller (two uniforms per sample, no cached spare) because `std::normal_distribution` is not specified bitwise. Tests: `save_restore_bitwise`, `save_restore_bitwise_aero`, `servo_save_restore_bitwise`, `all_save_restore_clone_bitwise` (mid-run restore off every sample / firmware tick, `all.xml`: every device type in one free-floating quad), `all_bus_log_and_replay`, `all_free_quad_runs_without_divergence`, `model_constants_follow_device_armature_and_are_idempotent`, and the unchanged `test_determinism` / `test_replay`.

Model constants: when an ESC or servo sets `dof_armature`, `bind()` refreshes every qpos0-derived constant (`dof_M0`, `dof_invweight0`, ...) once with `mj_setConst` on a scratch `mjData`: idempotent, safe on a reloaded model and after Spawn / Delete.

## Config transport

Devices are declared in the MJCF as `<custom><text name="napl.devices" data='{...json...}'/></custom>`. The JSON follows `schema/devices.fbs` (kept apart from `napl.fbs`, whose hash is in replay logs) and is parsed with `flatbuffers::Parser` (strict JSON: quoted names, an unknown field is an error) using the embedded binary schema, then copied into plain config structs; any error names the device (`napl.devices: device '<name>': ...`). `mj_saveModel` keeps custom text, so replay headers, `load_mujoco_bytes` and `clone` carry the configuration; every load path ends in the `MujocoSolver` constructor, which parses and binds. Device names become prims `/devices/<name>`; attributes and units are the tables at the end of `devices.cpp` (inputs are settable through `SetAttr`, validated before anything is written).
