# OSV: a 12.6 m offshore support vessel with a helipad, an RTK base station and a deck datalink

A crew-boat class vessel (outer dimensions of the Damen Fast Crew Supplier 1204) built from parts through `napl.design`: aluminium deep-V hull, wheelhouse, a 3 x 3 m marked
helipad on the aft deck, two electric Torqeedo Deep Blue 50i propulsion pods on 352 V batteries, a Pixhawk 6C + Raspberry Pi 5 + SiK radio avionics stack, an M8N navigation GNSS,
a u-blox ZED-F9P RTK base with its antenna on the mast, a VectorNav VN-100 motion unit (MRU) on the deck, and four navigation lights. It floats and moves in a seeded JONSWAP sea
(Hs 0.5 - 2.5 m, underway 0 - 8 kn), and `ship_scene` puts a lander (Skydio X500 class `x500v2`, `skydio`, or the `hopper`) on its pad or in the air above it, in one World with one sea
and one air device. The landers themselves, and the landing controllers, are other work; this is the ship, its sea, its pad and the relative-navigation aids they need.

```
tools/remote.sh run '.venv/bin/python examples/ship/build_design.py'                 # regenerate assemblies/osv.toml (DRC delta of every edit; no kernel)
tools/remote.sh run '.venv/bin/python -m pytest tests/python/test_ship.py -q'         # the tests (the first scene build routes ~40 wires: minutes, cached afterwards)
tools/remote.sh run --gpu '.venv/bin/python examples/ship/render_shots.py --out build/osv-shots --hs 1.5 --lander x500v2'   # stills: quarter, side, top (never committed)
```

```python

sc = ship_scene(outdir, lander="x500v2", lander_start="deck", hs=1.5, seed=3)    # lander_start="air", air_height=3.0 to hover over the pad

```

## The reference, and what is real

Outer dimensions: Damen Fast Crew Supplier 1204 (Damen Trading folio 07675: 12.60 x 3.80 x 1.70 m, draft 0.80 m, aluminium hull, 2 x Ultrajet 340, about 35 kn, 2.5 t deadweight,
909 bhp; https://medialibrary.damen.com/m/471c1f7842832c80/original/fcs-1204-for-sale-damen-trading-07675.pdf). The sheet publishes no displacement and no lines, so the underwater form, the
mass and every hydrodynamic coefficient are ESTIMATES (strip theory and rules of thumb, with the rationale in `parts/marine/generic-osv-hull-1260.toml`); the real boat is a 35 kn waterjet
craft and this one is a displacement-speed electric twin-screw version of it. Measured in the simulation (calm water, `test_ship.py`):

| quantity | value | basis |
|---|---|---|
| design mass | 10.30 t (hull 8.4 t of lumped structure and ballast, wheelhouse 0.9 t, pad 0.18 t, batteries 0.57 t, drives 0.17 t, rest below 0.1 t) | the hull mass is chosen to float at the estimated displacement |
| draft at rest | 0.700 m (the sheet's maximum is 0.80 m) | stacked-box hull, rho 1025 kg/m3 |
| speed | 4.0 m/s (7.9 kn) at 0.81 throttle, about 4.4 m/s at full; 1 m/s at 0.18 | `pilot.SPEED_THR`, measured under the heading loop |
| heave in Hs 1.5 m beam seas | 0.39 m rms (Hs / 4 = 0.375 m) | the hull follows the waves of Tp 6.1 s |
| roll in Hs 1.5 m beam seas | 5.6 deg rms (natural period 3 - 4 s, roll zeta 0.3 estimated) | wave slope 2.3 deg x a RAO of 2.4 |
| propulsion | 2 x (55 kW continuous, 66 kW peak, 1200 rpm max, 80 kg) on 2 x 38 kWh | Torqeedo Deep Blue 50i 1200 + Battery 40 data (preliminary) |

Real data (cited in the part files): the Damen outer dimensions, the Torqeedo pod power / rpm / mass and Battery 40 energy / voltage / mass / size, the ZED-F9P moving-base specification
(UBX-21023276: 8 / 10 Hz, 0.01 m + 1 ppm, convergence < 10 s, heading 0.4 deg), the VN-100 Rugged attitude accuracy (0.5 deg static, 1.0 deg dynamic), the Pixhawk 6C / Pi 5 / M8N / SiK
parts of the ASV. Estimated, and marked `fidelity = "estimated"` where they enter: hull mass and shape, all hydrodynamic coefficients (including the two lumped aft fins that stand in for the hull's
aft side force and are CALIBRATED to keep the bare hull's Munk yaw instability small), propeller open-water curve (B-series shape, P/D 1.0), battery cell count and internal resistance, the
ANN-MB-00 antenna and the nav-light data (retail listings were not reachable), the VN-100 noise and pin table, cable lengths, the 5 V / 12 V loads. Not modelled: waterjets, diesels,
rudders, the transom bracket of the drives, wave-induced loads on the helipad, spray and green water, and ship-air-wake (the air device is the lander's ordinary wind).

## Sea and pad

* **Sea.** One `Sea` device (`osv_sea`, rho 1025). `hs`, `tp` (default Pierson-Moskowitz 5 sqrt(Hs) s, at least 3.5 s), `wave_dir` (deg, the way the waves travel), optional `current`, all seeded.
  `jonswap_waves` builds the components with the kernel's own recipe but stops at 2.2 x the peak frequency instead of 3.5: the buoyancy-cell hull integrates the wave surface with no decay of the wave
  pressure with depth, so short waves of a few hull widths force the roll far too hard (9.8 deg rms at the 2.1 s roll resonance in Hs 1.5 m, measured, against 2 - 4 deg for a real boat); the omitted
  tail holds about 1 % of the variance. `w_hi=None` gives the kernel JONSWAP. The water plane `water` is drawn as the live wave mesh by the viewer and renderer.
* **Helipad.** The `landing` shape of `generic-helipad-deck-3m`: `contype 0 / conaffinity 1`, geom group 3, friction 0.9 / 0.005 / 0.0001. Vehicle geoms are `contype 1 / conaffinity 0`, so a lander's
  skids and legs collide with the deck and not with its own body; rays (group 3 is in the default `ray_groups`) hit it, so a downward rangefinder above the pad reads the deck distance. The markings
  (white border, ring and "H" bars) are render-only, in the part's visual mesh. Pad centre in the vessel frame: `PAD_CENTRE = (-3.9, 0, 1.75)` m (x forward, y to port, z up, origin mid-length at the keel),
  3.0 x 3.0 m. `POINTS` lists the MRU, the RTK base antenna, the navigation GNSS, the bow and the stern. `pad_pose(hull_pos, hull_quat)` gives the pad centre and normal.
* **Landers.** `lander_start="deck"` puts the lander's lowest collision point 2 mm over the pad (verified to rest there for `x500v2`, `skydio` and `hopper`); `"air"` hovers it `air_height` m above the pad centre.
  The vessel and the landers share a 1 ms timestep. The lander keeps its own devices (`<lander>_...`) and its own sensors; the environment device of the lander is replaced by the scene's one `air` device.

| device | what | attributes (units) |
|---|---|---|
| `osv_sea` | the sea | `time`, `eta_origin`, `current` (m/s), `waves` (a, k, w, theta, phi) |
| `osv_vessel` | hull physics | `submerged`, `wetted`, `eta`, `buoyancy`, `hydro` |
| `osv_esc1`, `osv_esc2` | port / starboard pod inverter | settable `throttle` (-1 .. 1, bidirectional), `omega` (rad/s) |
| `osv_rotor1`, `osv_rotor2` | the propellers (water) | `ge_ratio` = immersion |
| `osv_battery1`, `osv_battery2`, `osv_battery_house` | traction packs, 6S house pack | `voltage` (V), `current` (A), `soc` |
| `osv_imu`, `osv_mag`, `osv_gnss` | the autopilot's sensors (Pixhawk 6C, M8N at the cabin roof) | as the ASV: `gyro`, `accel`, `field`, `lat`, `lon`, `vel_ned` |
| `osv_mru_imu` | VN-100 on the deck (250 Hz) | `gyro` (rad/s), `accel` (m/s2) |
| `osv_gnss_base` | ZED-F9P base antenna at the mast top (10 Hz, 0.1 s latency, stand-alone 1.27 m per axis) | `lat`, `lon`, `vel_ned` |

Bodies: `/world/osv_hull` (pos, quat), plus the instance bodies below it; a lander's root is `/world/<lander>_<root>` (`x500v2_bottom`, `skydio_bottom`, `hopper_frame`).

## Relative navigation (`napl.cuas.relnav`)

These are python models (a datalink is a python channel in this repo, like `examples/asv/ais.py`) and not kernel devices: they carry no physics, need no schema or hash change, and every draw is seeded.
Frames: world ENU, SI, quaternions (w, x, y, z).

```python
from napl.cuas.relnav import RelGnss, DeckLink, DeckPredictor, deck_truth, deck_point, deck_point_at
rg = RelGnss(seed=1)                              # ZED-F9P moving-base pair (base on the vessel, rover on the lander)
fix = rg.step(t, base_pos, rover_pos)             # TRUE antenna positions in; -> RelFix(rel, vel, state, sigma_h, sigma_v, t_meas, t_avail, age) or None
link, pred = DeckLink(seed=2, rate_hz=25), DeckPredictor()
truth = deck_truth(t, hull_pos, hull_quat, truth)      # velocities by differencing (the kernel answers pos / quat of a body)
msg = link.step(t, truth)                         # newest delivered DeckMsg(pos, vel, quat, omega, roll, pitch, yaw, seq, age) or None
pred.add(msg, offset=PAD_CENTRE)                  # offset = the pad point in the body frame
t_f, p = pred.predict(2.0)                        # {"h": pad height (m), "roll", "pitch" (rad)} at 0.1 s spacing; None before 20 s of history
pred.next_window(horizon=8.0, duration=2.0, vz_max=0.15, tilt_max=0.052)    # start time of the next quiet period, or None
deck_point_at(msg, offset, t)                     # (position, velocity) of a deck point extrapolated from a message to time t
```

| model | rate / latency | errors | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| RTK relative position, FIX | 8 Hz, 0.125 s | 2 cm horizontal + 1 ppm, 3 cm vertical per axis, correlated over 3 s | ZED-F9P-02B data sheet: 0.01 m + 1 ppm CEP static, 8 Hz moving base, < 10 s convergence; dynamic sigma is 2 x (estimate) |
| FLOAT / SINGLE | same | 0.20 / 0.35 m, 1.0 / 1.6 m | estimates |
| corrections link | 2 Hz RTCM, Gilbert-Elliott bursts | > 2 s old: FLOAT, > 10 s: SINGLE, back: FIX after U(0.3, 1) x 10 s | estimates |
| deck datalink | 25 Hz (10 - 50), 80 ms, 3 % loss + bursts | attitude 0.5 deg rms (1.0 deg when the deck turns faster than 0.1 rad/s), rates 0.005 rad/s, heave and position 3 cm, velocity 0.05 m/s | VN-100 Rugged data sheet; link estimates |
| deck prediction | Yule-Walker AR(24) on 60 s at 10 Hz | RMS error at +2 s 0.23 m against a pad-height RMS of 0.38 m (0.61) in Hs 1.5 m beam seas (measured, 139 predictions; a modest gain, the sea is broad-banded) (`test_deck_predictor_skill_on_simulated_ship_motion`) | method, not a published figure |

## The autopilot (`pilot.py`)

`ShipPilot(sim)`: `set_heading(psi, speed)`, `set_route(waypoints, speed)`, `set_hold(target)`; `advance(sim)` steps 40 ms, runs the estimator on `osv_imu` / `osv_mag` / `osv_gnss` and writes the two ESC
throttles. Common throttle is the speed (feed-forward table plus PI on the estimated ground speed), differential throttle the yaw (PD on the heading with a rate-limited reference, the same loops as the ASV).
The bare hull is directionally unstable (Munk moment) and the lumped aft fins only make it neutral, so the heading loop is not optional. DP-lite has no lateral thruster: it points the bow at the target,
closes at a speed proportional to the distance and thrusts up-stream in a current, so it settles down-stream of the point within a few metres; it is not dynamic positioning.

## Limits

The hull is a stack of boxes (buoyancy cells), so the hydrostatics are right and the waterplane has steps; no hull lift, planing or slamming; the sea is linear Airy waves without orbital velocities, so
a lander's rotors see only the wind; no wake of the ship's superstructure. The RTK and datalink models are statistical, not a GNSS signal simulation. Everything marked estimated above needs replacing
with measurement before a number from it is used for a decision.
